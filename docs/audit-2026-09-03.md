# Security review, 2026-09-03

Scope: `contracts/src/`, before deployment. Six independent passes in three rounds, each given the
code and no access to the others' findings or to this file. The last round was a fix review and a
fresh adversarial pass run together; the fresh pass reached the same verdict as the fix review from
the opposite direction, and both found things the round before them had not. Tools run: gitleaks over history, semgrep, slither,
aderyn, plus `script/mutation_report.py`, which deletes each guard in turn and reruns the suite.

## Where the keys live, before any Solidity

| Key | Location | Can do | Hot? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner EOA | `contracts/.env` | everything, including `ownerCall` to drain the account | yes |
| Session key | `contracts/.env`, standing in for the enclave until step 5 | only what the fence allows | yes |
| Passkey | the authenticator on this machine | co-sign once the day's spend crosses the threshold | no |

**The fence bounds the agent, not the operator.** Anyone with this machine has the owner key and can
empty the account regardless of every check below. That is acceptable at five dollars and stops
being acceptable the moment it is not. There is no upgrade path and no admin key beyond the owner.

## What the fence claims

> An attacker who fully controls the session key cannot take more value out of this account than the
> configured budgets allow, in any asset, by any route.

Every finding below is a way that sentence was false.

## Fixed

### 1. A stranger could exhaust the daily budget. High.

`validateUserOp` accepted any caller. The EntryPoint is what binds an operation to its hash; a
direct caller binds nothing, so one observed `(opHash, signature)` pair could be replayed with
fabricated order fields until the budget was gone. No keys needed, gas only.

Fixed by refusing any caller that is not `op.sender`. `test/Griefing.t.sol`.

### 2. Gas was an unmetered ether withdrawal. Critical.

Every input to the EntryPoint's prefund is chosen by whoever signs the operation, and
`VesperAccount.validateUserOp` pays it in raw ether. Nothing measured it. A carrier order selling
one wei, asking for 4.5M gas at 10 gwei, with the submitter naming itself beneficiary, took **0.0466
ether for one wei of token budget**. Emptying the account's ether was bounded by nothing at all, and
the account holds more ether than WETH.

Fixed in two parts. Ceilings on each gas field bound one operation and keep the multiplication in
range; the operation's worst case cost is then metered against `limits[account][address(0)]`, an
ether budget the owner sets like any other. A paymaster is refused rather than reasoned about.
`test/Drains.t.sol`.

### 3. The price floor was written by the key assumed stolen. High.

CoW's only on-chain guarantee is the limit price in the order. Solver competition happens off chain
and is optional, and the presign event is on chain, so an order never needs the CoW orderbook at
all. The fence checked `buyAmount != 0` and nothing else, so selling the whole daily budget for one
wei cleared every bound. Demonstrated end to end against the deployed GPv2Settlement.

Fixed with `minBuyPerSell`, an owner-set worst acceptable price per pair. It is a manual price, not
an oracle, and it goes stale; stale refuses trades rather than allowing bad ones. A pair the owner
has not priced is not tradeable.

### 4. The biometric threshold looked at one order. Medium.

Sizing every order at exactly `biometricThreshold` moved the entire daily budget with zero
assertions ever evaluated. The threshold now counts the day, not the order.

The assertion was also bound to the order alone, so one approval was replayable by any later
operation carrying the same order. It now signs `keccak256(abi.encode(order, opHash))`, and opHash
carries the chain, the EntryPoint, the account and the nonce.

### 5. `SIG_OK` did not mean the call would go through. Low.

The fence checked four order fields; the account checked eight. An operation could pass the fence,
be charged against the budget during validation, and then revert. Six of those emptied a day with
nothing armed. The fence now checks all of them, plus an order that has already expired.

### 6. A token could be sold for itself. Medium.

Legal under every other check, and it settles as a burn. Refused in both places.

### 7. A fee was sell-side value nothing counted. Critical.

GPv2Settlement takes `sellAmount + feeAmount` from the account for a fill-or-kill sale, and every
control measured `sellAmount` alone. One wei of sellAmount with the balance in `feeAmount` passed
the per-trade cap, the daily cap and the threshold. Refused outright: CoW takes its fee in the price
now, so zero is also the only value that gets filled.

### 8. An order could stay armed until 2106. High.

A presignature is an instruction a solver may act on until `validTo`, which is a uint32, and the
caps meter orders as they are created rather than as they settle. A week of budgets could be armed
and settled in one block. Capped at thirty minutes, six times the deadline the enclave asks for.

Revoking the session key does not disarm an order that already exists. That is now two tests on the
fork, and the console has a button for it.

### 9. The gas fields decided how much of the reservation became a real transfer. Medium.

`_maxCost` read `maxFeePerGas` and never read `maxPriorityFeePerGas`, which is the word the
EntryPoint actually pays the beneficiary at: `min(maxFeePerGas, basefee + maxPriorityFeePerGas)`.
The bound held either way, but an honest operation realised about one percent of what it reserved
and a stolen one realised half, a ninety six fold difference from a word nothing looked at. Bounded
now, and `MAX_PRE_VERIFICATION_GAS` came down from 200,000 to 100,000 because every unit of it is
paid in full to whoever submits the bundle whatever the real L1 cost was.

### 10. Writing to the ledger slowed the refill. Medium.

The daily budget drained in proportion to what was recorded, so every write re-based the decay from
the amount left at that moment. A stolen key could keep the owner's budget suppressed indefinitely
by placing trades worth one unit: each one restarted the decay from a smaller number and it never
came back. It leaks at a fixed rate now, so the amount recovered between two moments depends only
on the time between them and a write costs nothing.

### 11. An order could sell nothing, and a price floor could round away. Low.

Selling zero cleared every cap and every floor, armed an order no solver would fill, charged the
full ether reservation and lengthened the sell token's own decay. Refused. Separately, the floor was
compared by dividing, which rounded the requirement down to the next whole unit; it is cross
multiplied now, which is exact, with `buyAmount` bounded to a uint128 to keep the product in range.

## Accepted, with the reason

### The daily cap admits twice its value in twenty four hours.

Both reviewers found this independently and both are right. The budget is a bucket of size D that
refills over a day: spend it, wait a day while it refills, spend it again, and 2D has left in a
little over a day. This is not a bug in the drain. **Every O(1) rate limiter with capacity D has the
same property**, including the window that resets and the token bucket, because the bound is
`D + D·T/W`. Bounding a rolling day to exactly D needs per-trade history, which is unbounded
storage.

What the drain does buy over the window that reset: at any instant no more than D is available, and
the budget only returns as fast as time passes. The version it replaced handed out the whole cap
twice, two seconds apart, if those two seconds crossed a boundary.

`VoicePolicy._spentToday` says this where someone will read it, and `script/Setup.s.sol` says to set
the cap to half of what you would accept losing.

### A failed execution still spends the budget.

`_recordSpend` runs during validation, before the call. The alternative is a post-execution hook the
account would have to be trusted to call. Finding 5 removed the interesting version of this by
making the fence's checks a superset of the account's, so the remaining cases are chain-level
failures rather than rules the fence forgot.

Related, and important to anyone reusing this module: writing storage during validation breaks the
ERC-4337 validation rules, so **this account cannot be submitted through a public bundler.** The
operator self-bundles, which is why it works.

### The biometric threshold is per token, and gas never asks for a face.

The threshold reads the same draining ledger the daily cap does, so twice the threshold moves per
rolling day without a face, and it is kept per sell token because the caps are and there is no price
on chain to normalise across them. N allowed tokens therefore give N independent allowances. Set the
threshold to what you want to approve by hand, divided by twice the number of allowed tokens; the
contract says so at the check.

The ether budget has no threshold at all, by construction. The passkey gates a sell token's spend
and gas is not a sell token, so the ether budget is a loss limit rather than something a human
approves. Gating it would mean no unattended operation, which is the product.

### The ether budget turns a drain into a freeze.

The budget is charged what an operation could cost, because at validation time nothing knows what it
did. On Base that over-meters by a large factor. So a stolen key can exhaust the ether budget in a
handful of maximum-gas operations, after which nothing trades until it refills, the owner's own
operator included. That is recoverable in one `ownerCall` and losing a day of trading beats losing
the ether, but it is a real change of failure mode rather than a pure improvement.

`maxFeePerGas` also has a ceiling and no floor, so an attacker who wants operations rather than
ether can declare one wei and make the ether budget non-binding on operation count. Nothing of value
leaves by that route, and it costs the attacker real gas out of their own pocket.

### `attestationHash` is recorded, never verified.

Verifying a TDX quote on chain is not affordable. The field is what the owner said they approved,
not a check, and both the contract and section 8 of the spec now say so instead of implying a
guarantee the code cannot make.

## Checked and clean

`WebAuthn.sol` was attacked hardest, because it was rewritten for gas after the first pass. The
`mload` of the first word is safe at every authenticator-data length from 0 to 36 and accepted at
37; `_base64url` matches `vm.toBase64URL` exhaustively at lengths 0 to 96 and under fuzzing; and
`_equalAt` returns false rather than reverting at every boundary including `2^256-1`. The
caller-supplied `typeIndex` and `challengeIndex` are not exploitable, and the argument for that is
written down rather than assumed: the closing quote in the needle is load-bearing, and there is a
test that fails if it is ever dropped.

Also clean: the validator and the executor cannot decode different bytes (fuzzed); the ABI decoder
genuinely rejects wide `uint128`, wide `uint48` and dirty `bool` calldata rather than masking them;
a stranger cannot make the validator answer about somebody else's account; `0`/`1` is the correct
packed `validationData` and forwarding it verbatim is right; a malicious allowlisted ERC-20 is never
called; `TYPE_HASH` matches CoW's type string; ecrecover returning zero is rejected; upper-half `s`
is rejected; P-256 fails closed when the precompile is absent; `receiver` is pinned to the account;
fill-or-kill cannot refill; no EIP-1271 back door; and no secret has ever been committed.

Slither reports no high or medium findings. Aderyn's one high is a false positive: the flagged
`abi.encodePacked` builds a string that is compared, never hashed. Its `ecrecover` malleability flag
is answered by the explicit `s` bound two lines below.

## What the fence is worth, stated plainly

With a session key fully compromised from the first block, and the owner key and passkey intact, an
attacker can take: up to twice `dailyCap` of each allowed token per day, at no worse than the
owner's configured price, with a passkey demanded once that token's day crosses the threshold; and
up to twice the ether budget per day in gas, with no passkey. They can also stop the account
trading for a day by exhausting the ether budget. Nothing else, by any route found in six passes.

Measured rather than argued: the worst rolling twenty four hours came out at 1.993 times `dailyCap`,
the most that can be armed at once at 1.019 times, and the deployed EntryPoint never charged the
account above what `_maxCost` recorded.
